Project 1. Decision-aware strawberry disease scouting using public datasets

Feasibility assessment

Very strong. This is locally relevant to Florida and has usable public strawberry disease datasets. UF/IFAS has a

Strawberry Advisory System that supports fungicide timing for anthracnose and Botrytis fruit rot, so the practical

decision is clear: find symptoms/risk zones and support scouting/spray decisions rather than simply classify a leaf

image.

Grower pain point and practical output

Growers need frequent scouting for diseases such as Botrytis fruit rot, anthracnose, powdery mildew, angular leaf

spot, blossom blight, and fruit rot. The pain point is early detection and prioritization: which rows/plants need

attention, whether symptoms are spreading, and whether conditions are consistent with disease risk.

Public datasets and resources

Strawberry Disease Detection Dataset: 2,500 images with segmentation annotations for seven strawberry

disease classes, including powdery mildew leaf/fruit, gray mold, anthracnose fruit rot, leaf spot, angular leaf spot,

and blossom blight. Useful for object detection, semantic segmentation, and instance segmentation.

Roboflow Strawberry Disease Detection Dataset: Approximately 4,918 open-source images with objectdetection style labels. Useful for YOLO-style baselines and deployment demos.

Mendeley hybrid strawberry disease dataset: A newer instance-segmentation resource extending earlier

strawberry disease datasets. Useful for robustness evaluation and dataset-merging experiments.

PlantVillage / PlantDoc / Plant Pathology FGVC datasets: Not strawberry-specific, but useful for pretraining,

cross-dataset generalization, VLM prompting, and disease representation learning.

Potential technical solution

• Baseline: train YOLOv8/YOLOv10/RT-DETR for detection and Mask2Former/YOLO-seg/SAM-assisted models

for segmentation.

• Foundation-model variant: use SAM/SAM2 to generate disease-region proposals, then classify regions with a

compact disease head or CLIP/VLM embeddings. Evaluate whether foundation-model proposals reduce

annotation dependence and improve cross-dataset transfer.

• Decision-aware output: report disease class, lesion area ratio, affected organ type, confidence, severity score,

and “scouting priority” instead of only top-1 class.

• Efficiency: distill a strong teacher into a MobileNet/YOLO-nano/RT-DETR-R18 model; evaluate latency and

memory on laptop CPU/GPU and ideally Jetson/phone-class settings.

• Optional weather-risk fusion without field data: simulate weather features or use public weather records to

create a proof-of-concept risk-fusion module; keep this as auxiliary because image datasets usually do not

include synchronized weather.

Experiments students can run without field data

• Within-dataset detection/segmentation: mAP, mask mAP, Dice/IoU, class-wise F1.

• Cross-dataset evaluation: train on one strawberry dataset, test on another; quantify domain shift.

• Few-shot adaptation: use 1/5/10/20 labeled examples per disease class and compare full fine-tuning,

LoRA/adapters, prompt tuning, and linear probing.

• Severity estimation: convert masks to lesion-area ratios and evaluate severity ranking accuracy if labels can be

derived or weakly annotated.

• Efficiency: FPS, model size, energy proxy, parameter count, FLOPs; compare full-size and compressed

models.

Publishable paper framing

A strong paper angle is: “Foundation-model assisted, edge-efficient strawberry disease segmentation and decisionaware scouting under cross-dataset domain shift.” The novelty is the combination of public field-like datasets,

lesion-level segmentation, few-shot/foundation adaptation, uncertainty/severity outputs, and deployable model compression.



Main risk and mitigation

Risk: public strawberry datasets may not perfectly match Florida cultivars and field conditions. Mitigation: make

cross-dataset robustness and few-shot adaptation the central research question, not Florida-only deployment.

Project 2. Citrus fruit counting and tree-health proxy mapping in HLB-affected

groves

Feasibility assessment

Very strong if framed as fruit counting/yield proxy and grove monitoring; weaker if framed as direct HLB diagnosis

from RGB alone. CitDet is especially relevant because it contains Florida citrus tree images from HLB-affected

fields in Fort Pierce and labels fruit on trees and ground.

Grower pain point and practical output

Citrus growers need yield estimates, fruit drop estimates, canopy/fruit-load monitoring, and efficient spray/resource

planning. HLB affects yield, fruit size/quality, mortality, and production cost, but image-only diagnosis should be

treated cautiously. A safer practical output is a tree-level health/yield proxy.

Public datasets and resources

CitDet: A public benchmark with 579 high-resolution citrus tree images, over 32,000 fruit bounding boxes,

collected in Fort Pierce, Florida in groves experiencing greening disease. It labels fruit on trees and ground,

making it useful for yield and fruit-drop related analysis.

Orange leaf HLB datasets: Smaller leaf-level datasets for healthy vs HLB/control classification. Useful only as

auxiliary evidence or representation transfer, not as a complete grove system.

Campaneta-Orange-Fruit UAV dataset: A 2026 UAV RGB/multispectral orange orchard dataset; useful for

aerial fruit detection and modality comparison if accessible.

General fruit-counting datasets: MinneApple, WSU Apple, VideoFruitCounting: Useful for developing

occlusion-aware counting and video tracking methods transferable to citrus.

Potential technical solution

• Baseline: train fruit detectors on CitDet using YOLO/RT-DETR/DINO-style detectors; evaluate separate labels

for on-tree and ground fruit.

• Tree-level aggregation: group detections by tree/image side; estimate fruit load, fruit-drop ratio, and uncertainty

intervals.

• Foundation-model support: use SAM/SAM2 for citrus/fruit masks, Grounding DINO for open-vocabulary fruit

localization, and CLIP/VLM captions for weak supervision; compare against supervised detectors.

• Health proxy: derive image-level metrics such as canopy density proxy, fruit load, ground-fruit ratio, and visible

stress score. Do not claim lab-confirmed HLB status unless labels support it.

• Efficiency: develop a real-time fruit counter using lightweight detection plus tracking; test

pruning/quantization/distillation.

Experiments students can run without field data

• Fruit detection: mAP50/mAP50-95, small-object AP, on-tree vs ground fruit AP.

• Counting: MAE/RMSE/MAPE per image/tree side; calibration of uncertainty intervals.

• Domain transfer: pretrain on apple fruit datasets, adapt to citrus; compare zero-shot, few-shot, and full finetuning.

• Occlusion robustness: stratify by canopy density, fruit size, lighting, and clustered fruits.
