Next steps, before touching architecture:
1. More aggressive augmentation
small rotations
color jitter
brightness/contrast
random crops
2. Weighted Cross Entropy
not because classes are imbalanced
because medium is hardest
**3. Regression instead of tertiles** <---
predict ICP concentration directly
then bin afterward

Of those three, the one most likely to give insight rather than just a few extra percentage points is regression on the raw ICP value. The behavior of Fold 2 makes me suspect the underlying problem is that the tertile boundaries are somewhat artificial relative to the visual signal.

That is, bins are based on ICP rank:
lowest 1/3 → low
middle 1/3 → medium
highest 1/3 → high

Nominal treatment	Range of Residue Density	Mean of values in range
400			0.047508538 - 0.097070081	0.074133052
600			0.072583814 - 0.21293537	0.116066217
800			0.076879462 - 0.269263945	0.127309529
1000			0.106250699 - 0.239437237	0.17785577


But the visual signal may not separate cleanly that way. For example, maybe the images only visually show:
low-ish residue vs high-ish residue
or maybe ICP changes continuously, so samples near the low/medium or medium/high boundary look almost identical.

Key idea:
Classification asks: Which arbitrary bin is this image in?
Regression asks: What ICP value does this image correspond to?

That could be due to small data, label noise, uncontrolled lighting, leaf orientation, leaf surface variation, inconsistent spraying/deposition, or RGB simply not capturing the chemistry strongly enough.

Stratified 4-fold cross-validation was run using mg/cm² as a continuous regression target. The model achieved Val MAE = 0.0594 ± 0.0115 mg/cm² and Val RMSE = 0.0747 ± 0.0189 mg/cm². However, R² was negative across the cross-validation summary (-0.7629 ± 0.4653), and the model did not outperform a mean-value baseline MAE (baseline = 0.0451 ± 0.0046). Pearson correlation was weak and unstable overall (0.1707 ± 0.4265), although Fold 3 showed a stronger positive relationship (r ≈ 0.715). Scatter plots showed that the model produces varied predictions rather than collapsing to a constant value, but the predictions are not consistently aligned with ground-truth residue density. These results suggest that the model may capture weak visual signal in some splits, but current performance is not yet sufficient to claim reliable generalization.
